# Systematic Literature Review Search Strategy Guide

## Overview
This guide provides a structured approach to developing and executing search strategies for systematic literature reviews (SLR). Based on information literacy principles, it will help you find relevant, high-quality sources efficiently.

## The 7-Step Search Process

### 1. 📋 Analyze Your Topic

**Before starting your search:**
- Define your research question using PICO/SPIDER framework
- Gather background knowledge from:
  - Encyclopedia entries
  - Review articles
  - AI tools (following institutional guidelines)
  - Wikipedia (for initial understanding only)

**Key Questions:**
- What do you already know about the topic?
- What specific knowledge gaps need filling?
- What is your time frame and scope?

### 2. 🎯 Develop Your Search Strategy

**Create a structured approach:**

#### Identify Core Concepts
- Extract 3-4 main concepts from your research question
- Find synonyms and related terms
- Consider broader and narrower terms
- Translate key terms to English (most databases are English-based)

#### Boolean Logic
Use these operators to combine search terms:
- **AND**: Narrows results (intersection)
- **OR**: Expands results (union) 
- **NOT**: Excludes terms

#### Example Search Strategy Template:
```
Research Question: "What are the effects of real-time speech translation on communication effectiveness?"

Block 1 (Technology): 
real-time OR simultaneous OR instant OR live
AND
speech translation OR voice translation OR spoken language translation

Block 2 (Communication):
communication OR interaction OR conversation OR dialogue
AND
effectiveness OR quality OR accuracy OR comprehension

Block 3 (Context):
multilingual OR cross-language OR international OR intercultural
```

### 3. 🔍 Select Search Tools

Choose appropriate databases based on your field:

#### Academic Databases
- **PubMed**: Medical and life sciences
- **IEEE Xplore**: Engineering and technology
- **ACM Digital Library**: Computer science
- **Scopus**: Multidisciplinary
- **Web of Science**: Multidisciplinary
- **Google Scholar**: Broad coverage (use with caution)

#### Specialized Resources
- **arXiv**: Preprints in physics, mathematics, computer science
- **ResearchGate**: Academic networking and papers
- **DBLP**: Computer science bibliography
- **CiteSeerX**: Computer and information science

#### Gray Literature
- Conference proceedings
- Technical reports
- Theses and dissertations
- Government publications

### 4. 🔎 Execute Your Search

#### Search Techniques

**Quick and Dirty Search:**
- Initial exploration using broad terms
- Good for understanding the field
- Use Google Scholar or general databases

**Precision Search:**
- Use specific combinations of terms
- Apply filters (date, document type, language)
- Use database-specific subject headings

**Citation Chaining:**
- Follow references from key papers
- Use "cited by" features
- Build from seminal works

**Block Search Method:**
1. Search each concept block separately
2. Combine blocks with AND
3. Refine as needed

#### Search Tips
- Use quotation marks for exact phrases: "machine translation"
- Use wildcards for variations: translat* (finds translate, translation, translator)
- Use field-specific searches: author:, title:, abstract:
- Set date ranges appropriate for your topic
- Consider language limitations

### 5. 📊 Evaluate Your Results

**Quality Indicators:**
- Publication venue (journal impact factor, conference ranking)
- Author credentials and affiliations
- Citation count and recency
- Peer review status
- Methodology rigor

**Relevance Assessment:**
- Does it directly address your research question?
- Is the methodology appropriate?
- Is the sample size adequate?
- Are the findings clearly presented?

**Managing Results:**
- Too many results? Add more specific terms with AND
- Too few results? Use broader terms with OR, remove restrictions
- Use database filters: publication date, document type, subject area

### 6. 📚 Acquire Materials

**Access Strategies:**
- Use institutional library access
- Check for open access versions
- Request through interlibrary loan
- Contact authors directly
- Use legal preprint servers

**Organization:**
- Use reference management software (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote)
- Create systematic folder structures
- Document search strategies for reproducibility
- Keep track of which databases you've searched

### 7. 🎓 Assess Quality and Credibility

**Source Evaluation Criteria:**

#### Authority
- Author expertise and credentials
- Institutional affiliation
- Publication venue reputation

#### Accuracy
- Peer review process
- Methodology transparency
- Data availability
- Reproducible results

#### Currency
- Publication date relevance
- Most recent findings
- Historical context when needed

#### Coverage
- Comprehensive literature review
- Adequate sample sizes
- Appropriate scope

**Red Flags:**
- Predatory journals
- Lack of peer review
- Missing methodology details
- Extraordinary claims without evidence
- Conflicts of interest not disclosed

## SLR-Specific Considerations

### PRISMA Guidelines
Follow PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) standards:
- Document your search strategy
- Report number of records at each stage
- Explain inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Provide flowchart of study selection

### Search Documentation
Keep detailed records:
- Database searched
- Search terms used
- Date of search
- Number of results
- Filters applied

### Multiple Reviewers
- Have searches validated by second reviewer
- Use standardized screening forms
- Calculate inter-rater reliability
- Resolve conflicts through discussion

## Tips for Success

1. **Start Early**: Literature searching is iterative and time-consuming
2. **Be Systematic**: Document everything for reproducibility
3. **Stay Flexible**: Refine your strategy based on what you find
4. **Seek Help**: Consult librarians and subject matter experts
5. **Use Technology**: Leverage reference managers and search alerts
6. **Quality over Quantity**: Focus on relevant, high-quality sources
7. **Regular Updates**: Set up alerts for new publications in your area

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Relying on a single database
- Using only English language sources when multilingual sources exist
- Stopping at the first good result
- Not documenting search strategies
- Ignoring gray literature
- Failing to update searches before finalizing
- Over-relying on citation counts alone
