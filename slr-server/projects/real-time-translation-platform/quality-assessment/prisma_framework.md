## Quality Assessment Overview

### Purpose
To systematically evaluate the methodological quality and risk of bias in included studies to inform evidence synthesis and interpretation.

### Framework Selected: PRISMA

The PRISMA framework is used because:
- It's widely recognized in systematic reviews
- Applicable to diverse study designs
- Provides transparent quality indicators
- Facilitates comparison across studies

---

## Quality Assessment Tool

### Study Characteristics Assessment

#### 1. Study Design Appropriateness
**Question:** Is the study design suitable for answering the research question?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 2 | Empirical study with appropriate design for RQ (e.g., comparative implementation study, benchmark) |
| 1 | Empirical study but design limitations for RQ (e.g., single case study) |
| 0 | Theoretical paper, no empirical validation, or inappropriate design |

#### 2. Sample/Scope Adequacy
**Question:** Is the sample size or scope adequate for the claims?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 2 | Multiple systems, platforms, or comprehensive scope; n > 100 if applicable |
| 1 | Limited sample or scope; n = 10-100 if applicable |
| 0 | Single case; n < 10; or scope too narrow for claims |

#### 3. Clarity of Methods
**Question:** Are methods clearly described for reproducibility?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 2 | Methods fully described; reproducible by other researchers |
| 1 | Methods mostly clear but some details missing |
| 0 | Methods vague or incomplete |

#### 4. Statistical/Analytical Rigor
**Question:** Are analyses appropriate and well-executed?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 2 | Appropriate statistical/analytical methods; proper interpretation |
| 1 | Acceptable methods but some limitations; minor interpretation issues |
| 0 | No statistics; inappropriate methods; or misinterpretation |

#### 5. Reporting Transparency
**Question:** Is reporting complete and transparent?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 2 | Complete reporting; limitations discussed; conflicts of interest disclosed |
| 1 | Mostly complete but some gaps; limitations mentioned |
| 0 | Incomplete reporting; limitations not discussed |

#### 6. Bias Management
**Question:** Are potential sources of bias addressed?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 2 | Potential biases identified and addressed; limitations discussed |
| 1 | Some bias considerations but incomplete |
| 0 | No consideration of bias; no limitation discussion |

### Quality Score Calculation

```
Total Quality Score = Sum of Items 1-6
Range: 0-12 points
```

**Quality Categories:**
- **High Quality (10-12):** Included despite any minor issues
- **Moderate Quality (7-9):** Generally included; limitations noted
- **Low Quality (0-6):** Careful consideration; may exclude if severe issues

## Risk of Bias Assessment

### Study-Level Risk of Bias

#### Selection Bias
- **Question:** Are included participants/systems representative?
- **Risk Levels:** Low / Moderate / High / Unclear
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Low:** Population clearly described; selection criteria explicit; representative sample
  - **Moderate:** Some selection bias likely but minimal impact
  - **High:** Clear selection bias affecting results
  - **Unclear:** Insufficient information

#### Performance Bias
- **Question:** Could intervention/system implementation differ systematically?
- **Risk Levels:** Low / Moderate / High / Unclear
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Low:** Implementation standardized and clearly described
  - **Moderate:** Some variation in implementation; mostly documented
  - **High:** Significant implementation variation not addressed
  - **Unclear:** Implementation details not provided

#### Detection Bias
- **Question:** Could outcome measurement differ systematically?
- **Risk Levels:** Low / Moderate / High / Unclear
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Low:** Outcome measures clearly defined; consistent application
  - **Moderate:** Measurements mostly standardized; minor inconsistencies
  - **High:** Subjective measures; inconsistent application
  - **Unclear:** Outcome measurement not clearly described

#### Attrition Bias
- **Question:** Are data complete for all participants/systems studied?
- **Risk Levels:** Low / Moderate / High / Unclear
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Low:** Complete data; no dropouts or clearly addressed
  - **Moderate:** < 10% data loss; addressed
  - **High:** > 10% data loss; not addressed
  - **Unclear:** Follow-up status unclear

#### Reporting Bias
- **Question:** Are results selectively reported?
- **Risk Levels:** Low / Moderate / High / Unclear
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Low:** All outcomes reported; protocol available/followed
  - **Moderate:** Some selective reporting; minor concerns
  - **High:** Selective reporting evident; outcomes changed
  - **Unclear:** No protocol available

#### Other Bias
- **Question:** Are there any other significant bias sources?
- **Risk Levels:** Low / Moderate / High / Unclear
- **Examples:** 
  - Funding bias (industry funding for commercial system)
  - Author bias (paper by system developers)
  - Publication bias (positive results only)

## Quality-Based Sensitivity Analysis Plan

### Planned Sensitivity Analyses:

1. **By Quality Category:**
   - Synthesis with all papers
   - Synthesis with high-quality papers only (score 10-12)
   - Compare results between analyses

2. **By Risk of Bias:**
   - Synthesis including all bias risk levels
   - Synthesis with only low-risk papers
   - Note any substantive differences

3. **Subgroup Analysis:**
   - By study design type
   - By publication year
   - By technology focus (neural vs. traditional)

### Rationale:
Sensitivity analyses help determine if conclusions are robust to quality variations or driven by lower-quality studies.
